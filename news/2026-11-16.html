MIT teaches a robot to notice its own fuzzy memories, and fix them

MIT researchers built a system that flags the uncertain entries in a robot's visual memory, such as a defibrillator mislabeled as a fire extinguisher, and pulls a couple of extra stored snapshots to settle the ambiguity rather than trusting a single error-prone guess. In their reported tests it resolved the uncertainty for about 92% of the objects a robot had seen, using just two extra captions per object; the work will be presented at the Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems.

DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 reads DNA cell by cell, and gets much better at spotting disease-linked variants

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2, trained on more than 100 million single-cell profiles across 68 tissues, which predicts how a DNA variant affects one specific tissue or cell type instead of an average across all of them. DeepMind reports it identifies nearly twice as many variants that change gene expression in cells compared with its previous model, a real gain for researchers trying to connect DNA changes to disease. It's free for non-commercial research, and DeepMind is upfront that it isn't validated for clinical use yet.

Google's Language Explorer puts 7,000 of the world's languages on one interactive map

Google Research and Google DeepMind launched Language Explorer, an open platform built on the 1,000 Languages Initiative's LinguaMeta dataset, letting anyone search roughly 7,000 languages and 159 scripts by name, region, speaker numbers, or endangerment status. It's also on public display at the Museum of Communication in Berlin, a nice reminder that AI can help document and keep human languages visible instead of flattening them.
