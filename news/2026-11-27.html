The EU's ban on AI "nudifier" apps takes effect next week, and women MEPs are pushing to make it real

From 2 December 2026, the EU bans apps and websites built to generate non-consensual sexual images of real people, often called "nudifier" tools. More than 50 members of the European Parliament say they've been targeted by this kind of abuse themselves, and 119 of them signed a letter this autumn urging the European Commission and national governments to have real enforcement authorities ready for day one, not just a rule on paper.

Starting the same week, older AI tools sold in the EU must mark their output as AI-made

Under Article 50 of the EU AI Act, providers of generative AI systems that were already on the market before August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to add machine-readable marking that identifies their text, images, and audio as AI-generated. The rule exists so that anyone who runs into AI content, a reader, a platform, or a detection tool, has something real to check it against instead of guessing, and providers who miss the deadline risk fines of up to EUR 15 million or 3% of global turnover.

An AI platform at UCLA just found a promising new pairing against treatment-resistant prostate cancer

UCLA researchers built MAESTRO, an AI platform described in the journal Advanced Science, to screen natural compounds against the biological pathways that let prostate tumours resist treatment. It narrowed 3,592 candidates down to 150, tested the most promising 23 in the lab, and found that pairing the common supplement berberine with the UCLA-developed drug enzalutamide worked better against prostate cancer cells than either one alone. The result is still confined to the lab, and it's a nice example of AI doing the unglamorous sifting that frees researchers to chase the leads worth testing.
