AI-cloned voices are making swatting calls harder for 911 dispatchers to catch

NPR reports that swatters are increasingly using AI-generated voices, complete with natural filler words and faked background noise, to make hoax emergency calls sound convincing. A survey of public-safety professionals found roughly 14% of dispatchers say they've already been targeted this way. Dispatchers still send help to every call regardless, and trainers are now building techniques, like asking callers hyper-specific questions an AI can't look up, to catch the fakes.

Source: wamc.org (NPR) →

The EU's AI content-labelling duty is live, and a December deadline brings older tools into scope too

Article 50 of the EU AI Act has required generative AI providers to make their output machine-readable as AI-made, and required anyone deploying a deepfake to disclose it, since 2 August 2026, per the European Commission's own guidance. Legal trackers report that AI systems already on the market before that date get a short grace period, with the machine-readable marking duty extending to 2 December 2026, a date worth knowing if you ship an AI tool into Europe.

Source: digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu →

Apple opens a 200,000-person study into how people actually use AI for health questions

Apple and the clinical AI platform OpenEvidence launched the Apple Health AI Usage Study on 15 November, open to up to 200,000 US adults with an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone. It's built to learn what actually builds trust and understanding when people ask AI about their health. Apple says study data won't be used to train models, and participants choose what, if anything, they share.
